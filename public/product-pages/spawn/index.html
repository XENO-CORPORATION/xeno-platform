Why a canvas

A long project fills the window.

Every turn adds to the agent’s context. Past a point, it has to compact and carry on from a summary.

Compaction loses two things at once.

The agent loses detail, working from a summary of its own history. You lose the trail: a 74 MB linear log is not something a person scrolls.

The canvas keeps what the window drops.

The compaction boundary is itself a node, with edges from everything it summarised. The detail stays one click from the summary that replaced it.

Two channels

Files go through the workspace. Messages go on the wire.

Conflating the two is what makes agents collide: two overwrite each other while both report success. The workspace is governed by a claim lock; isolation is declared per agent.

Boundaries

What it is not

Not a workflow authoring toolXENO Workflow owns triggered, repeating graphs. Spawn invokes the same nodes once and keeps the output as a record. XENO Workflow
Not a second agent hostThere is one authority for running agents. Spawn renders the graph that host owns.
Not an isolation substrateConfinement comes from xeno-use.
Not an editorYour job here is to spawn, steer, review and land.
Not a whiteboardEvery node has a spawner. Anything placed without a causal parent isn’t part of the record.
